Classical
functions and cadences
A tonal centre and three families: home (I, vi, iii) → departure (IV, ii) → tension (V, vii°) → home. Harmony is a cycle.
Major triad
The 3rd decides major or minor; the 5th stabilises and can be left out.
Minor triad
Minor 3rd: a sweet, darker home.

Impressionism
colour, silence, suspension
Each sound stands like an object in a garden, with silence (ma) between as material. Chords are colours left unresolved. Roots: Debussy, Messiaen, Japanese aesthetics.
Lydian light: maj7(♯11) without the 5th
Major 7th and ♯11 in open voicing: the chord floats. The 5th is left out on purpose, so it doesn't anchor a centre.
Semitone + tritone (the E♭–E–A cell)
The cell Takemitsu calls “S–E–A” (Es–E–A): a semitone spread into a minor 9th, plus a tritone. Friction that shines without asking for resolution.
Whole-tone cloud
Four notes of the whole-tone scale: none pulls toward another, the harmony hangs like mist.
Dominant that doesn't resolve (7♭9)
Dominant texture without a destination. All notes belong to the half-step–whole-step octatonic scale.
Shift by a minor 3rd
The octatonic scale repeats every minor 3rd: the chord moves, the colour stays.
Bass a semitone down
Sliding under a still layer; no V–I.
Tritone relation
The most distant relation: two colours that share their tritone, without direction.

Modernism
rhythm, blocks, dry brilliance
Energy comes from rhythm and the clash of clean blocks. Everything dry and clipped; harmony stands inside the block, and “progress” is the cut.

The “Augurs” chord from The Rite of Spring: F♭ major below, E♭7 above. In the score the strings hammer it with irregular accents.

Perfect 4ths stacked on each other: a dry, archaic sound that declares neither major nor minor.

Byzantine

One melodic line and its ison (drone). Whatever is added echoes the line without harmonising it. In the orchestra it is a stylisation that respects the chant.
In the Byzantine landscape chords don't belong to the idiom: hold the ison and put the melody above it.
